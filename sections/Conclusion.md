# Conclusion

We asked how the cognitive abilities of language models can be organized around a general factor. Our answer comes from factor analyses of \textcolor{red}{13,230} published evaluation scores covering 1,618 models and 456 benchmarks, triangulated so that no single treatment of the missing data carries the finding. \textcolor{red}{Across the imputations that pass our quality filter, a general latent factor accounts for 4\% to 76\% of the variance in the summed score ($\omega_h$ from 0.04 to 0.76) and 11\% to 52\% of the common variance (ECV).} Domain-similar benchmarks are only occasionally located close together in vector space, and benchmarks with high $G$-loading do not share a common theme and differ from the benchmarks the field treats as standard measures of intelligence. We conclude that a hierarchical \textcolor{red}{structure} of machine intelligence, as commonly but only implicitly assumed in the field, is \textcolor{red}{not clearly identifiable from this corpus,} given the \textcolor{red}{hard-to-interpret} factor structure. Correlations arise from unintuitive and unpredictable common features. \textcolor{red}{Whether a non-hierarchical network of abilities describes these data better is left for future work.}
